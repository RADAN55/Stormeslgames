Before you play

What makes a game EL-friendly

A game helps English learners when it makes them produce the language. Fun is the delivery system; the frame is the lesson.

They produce it

The point is scored by saying, writing or explaining the term, not just recognizing it.

A frame is posted

"I think ___ because ___." The frame is the scaffold; the game is the reason to use it.

It repeats

Same structure, new content each week. Students learn the rules once and spend their energy on content.

Stakes stay low

Team scoring, partner talk before sharing, and "phone a friend" options protect newcomers.

Home language is welcome

Partners who share a language can plan in it, then report out in English.

There's a visual

A picture, object, gesture or diagram sits beside every term.

Level key (ELPA21)

EEmerging, Levels 1–2. Newcomers and early learners. Give them pictures, word banks, pointing or gesture answers, and a home-language partner.
PProgressing, Levels 3–4. Students who talk but miss academic language. Give them sentence frames, a word bank and time to rehearse.
AAll levels. Built-in tiers: the same game with a different frame for each student.

Prep key

NonePlay it today, with nothing to make.
LowAbout 10 minutes: a list, a slide or a few strips.
